My original skeleton agent finished eighth out of eight agents in the seeded baseline comparison, averaging a loss of $1,008.68 per episode, with a 95% confidence interval of −$1,015.10 to −$1,002.26. My final agent finished first out of eight, averaging $21,477.66 per episode, with an interval of $20,618.55 to $22,336.77. Across ten episodes, total profit changed from −$10,086.80 to $214,776.57, and the final agent completed all ten full-length episodes. I would not give the layout algorithm credit for that entire turnaround. Replacing random hiring with five workers and one manager prevented early bankruptcy; with that same staffing and no layout changes, profit already averaged $21,081.98. The layout added $395.67 per episode, about 1.88%, on the held-out seeds. Its paired 95% difference interval was −$216.82 to $1,008.17, so this small test does not establish a reliable positive layout effect. The final agent also beat intelligent_queue by only $309.62 on average; first place here is a sample ranking, not proof that it will always win.

I replaced random swaps with a greedy search that uses observed product accesses and actual aisle distances to the delivery bays. Each proposed swap evaluates both products, including the item displaced from the closer slot, and handles products with multiple storage copies. Swaps require reachable storage endpoints, an idle manager, a manageable queue, and a positive predicted improvement; a cooldown helps prevent repeatedly reversing a move. I tested frequency-only placement, a co-occurrence version, and a version charging for manager travel. On the three development seeds, their mean profits were $21,908.97, $21,871.86, and $20,415.30, respectively, so I selected frequency-only before testing ten new seeds. More complicated scoring did not automatically perform better. Next, I would estimate demand from arriving orders rather than completed accesses, since hard-to-retrieve products can look less popular than they are. I would also test demand changes, more warehouse layouts, and larger seed sets, then compare any staffing or routing changes separately so I can tell what actually helped.
