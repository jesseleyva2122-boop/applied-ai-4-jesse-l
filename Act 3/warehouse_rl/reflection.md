# Warehouse Staffing and Pickup Reflection
Jesse Leyva

My previous layout agent was already first out of eight agents, so improving the warehouse meant increasing profit rather than just moving up the rankings. In ten new episodes, its average profit was $21,922.18, with a 95% confidence interval of $21,020.08 to $22,824.27. My final agent stayed first out of eight and averaged $45,084.78, with an interval of $44,171.36 to $45,998.20. Total profit across the ten episodes increased from $219,221.78 to $450,847.80, a 105.66% improvement. Average completion rate also rose from 40.06% to 70.41%. I used separate development seeds before testing the final version, and each policy received a fresh environment. That matters because the original benchmark reused an order generator across episodes. The simulator also changes demand when service improves, so these results measure the whole warehouse's response, rather than performance on exactly the same list of orders.

For staffing, I replaced the fixed five-worker target with decisions based on waiting orders, employee availability, estimated service time, and the extra payroll a hire would create. I added cooldowns to prevent constant hiring and firing, protected the manager from the simulator's firing rule, and avoided late hires that would have little time to earn their wages back. For pickups, I matched available workers to orders using estimated aisle travel, order value, age, and deadlines. I also fixed the point where the simulator automatically assigned newly idle workers, which had initially bypassed my pickup decisions. These algorithms depend on each other: a manager enables product moves, better product locations shorten estimated trips, and enough workers turn those shorter trips into completed orders. However, the separate tests showed staffing produced most of the improvement. The combined agent averaged $982.82 more than staffing alone, but that gain's confidence interval included zero, so I cannot claim a consistent routing advantage yet. Next, I would estimate travel time from completed trips and account more directly for congestion before adding more workers.
